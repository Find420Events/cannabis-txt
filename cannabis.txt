======================================================================
1. PURPOSE
======================================================================

cannabis.txt is a proposed, openly usable, human-readable reference file
that describes the cannabis industry, its terminology, participants,
business categories, activities, and relationships.

Its purpose is to provide publishers with a consistent way to explain
their own cannabis-related subject matter to people, search engines,
research tools, and AI systems that choose to retrieve and interpret it.

This specification is an informational convention. It does not create a
guaranteed ranking signal, crawler directive, AI training mechanism, or
endorsement by any search engine or AI provider.

======================================================================
2. DESIGN PRINCIPLES
======================================================================

- Open: any website, organization, researcher, or developer may
  publish a compatible file without permission or payment.
- Human-readable: plain text is the required baseline.
- Consistent: use the defined field names and category vocabulary
  where practical.
- Extensible: publishers may add clearly named fields and categories.
- Jurisdiction-aware: legal and licensing claims must identify the
  relevant jurisdiction and effective date when known.
- Verifiable: distinguish confirmed facts, publisher statements,
  estimates, and unknown information.
- Privacy-conscious: never expose personal, confidential, or sensitive
  information about individuals.
- Non-exclusive: no publisher owns the industry taxonomy or may imply
  that compatibility requires using its services.

======================================================================
3. CONFORMANCE
======================================================================

A compatible file SHOULD:

- Be available at /cannabis.txt on the publishing domain.
- Use UTF-8 plain text.
- Identify its version and status.
- Explain the publisher's relationship to the information.
- Use recognizable section headings.
- Link to authoritative public pages where useful.
- Distinguish the publisher's own information from general
  definitions.
- Keep content current and correct outdated information promptly.

A compatible file MUST NOT:

- Claim that publication guarantees search ranking or AI citations.
- Misrepresent licensing, legal status, medical efficacy, or product
  safety.
- Present one jurisdiction's law as universal.
- Include credentials, private user data, or non-public records.
- Imply certification or endorsement by a regulator without evidence.

======================================================================
4. CORE INDUSTRY DEFINITION
======================================================================

The cannabis industry is an ecosystem of organizations, businesses,
professionals, consumers, technologies, products, services, research,
and activities associated with cannabis and hemp.

Depending on jurisdiction and context, the ecosystem may include
cultivation, processing, manufacturing, testing, distribution, retail,
delivery, education, advocacy, events, media, technology, professional
services, and ancillary suppliers.

Cannabis and hemp may be regulated differently. Definitions and legal
thresholds vary by jurisdiction. Publishers should not assume that a
single definition applies worldwide.

======================================================================
8. JURISDICTION AND REGULATORY CONTEXT
======================================================================

Cannabis laws differ by country, state, province, territory, tribal
jurisdiction, county, and municipality.

When making a legal or licensing statement, publishers SHOULD provide:

- Jurisdiction name and level.
- Relevant agency or official source.
- Effective date or last-verified date.
- A link to the official source.
- A clear distinction between law, guidance, and interpretation.

Do not use labels such as "licensed" or "legal" as unqualified marketing
claims. A directory listing or user-submitted profile is not proof of
authorization.

======================================================================
9. ENTITY AND RELATIONSHIP MODEL
======================================================================

Where relevant, describe public relationships such as:

- Organization operates brand.
- Organization organizes event.
- Brand exhibits at event.
- Vendor provides service to organization.
- Venue hosts event.
- Speaker participates in event.
- Event occurs in jurisdiction.
- Organization publishes public resource.

Use direct links to the relevant public entity pages. Do not fabricate
relationships or imply endorsement from a simple listing.

======================================================================
10. RECOMMENDED FILE FORMAT
======================================================================

The baseline format is UTF-8 plain text with Markdown-compatible
headings and links. Publishers may include simple key-value metadata.

Recommended metadata:

Title: Cannabis Industry Knowledge File
Version: 1.0.0
Status: Proposed
Publisher: [Organization name]
Canonical URL: [URL]
Last-Updated: [YYYY-MM-DD]
Language: en
Contact: [Public contact URL]

======================================================================
12. DISCOVERY AND LINKING
======================================================================

A publisher may link to its cannabis.txt from:

- Its homepage or About page.
- Its public documentation.
- Its sitemap or developer resources.
- Relevant public industry pages.

The file may be referenced in robots.txt as a comment or ordinary
sitemap-adjacent documentation link, but no special crawler behavior
should be assumed. Do not invent a robots.txt directive for it.

======================================================================
13. MAINTENANCE AND VERSIONING
======================================================================

Use semantic versioning:

- MAJOR: incompatible changes to definitions or required fields.
- MINOR: backward-compatible additions.
- PATCH: clarifications and corrections.

Include a last-updated date. Keep a public changelog where practical.
Old versions may be archived for transparency, but the current file
should be easy to identify.

======================================================================
14. GOVERNANCE
======================================================================

This document is a proposed community specification initially published
by Find420.Events. Publication does not make it an industry-approved or
regulator-approved standard.

Community participation may include:

- Public issue reporting.
- Proposed taxonomy changes.
- Discussion of definitions.
- Implementation examples.
- Compatibility testing.
- Version review.

Changes should be documented, reviewed, and publicly versioned. No
single publisher should imply that it speaks for the entire industry.
